About

Bridge between biology and photonics

I am a second-year Ph.D. student in Bioengineering at Harvard SEAS (Jul 2024–present), with an M.S. in Biomedical Electronics and Bioinformatics and a B.S. in Life Science from National Taiwan University.

My research identity sits at the intersection of nanoscale imaging, photonics, nanomaterials, and semiconductor device physics. I am strongest when connecting physical mechanisms, optical instrumentation, and biological measurement into one coherent direction.

I have published in Nature Communications, ACS Photonics, and Gels, with ongoing work in laser-particle bioimaging, cathodoluminescent probes, and quantum photonic devices.

Research areas

Where my work lives

⬡

Nanoscale light–matter interaction

Studying how light, electrons, and nanomaterials interact at small length scales — from super-resolution microscopy to cathodoluminescence and energy transfer in lanthanide nanoparticle systems.

◎

Laser particles and photonic probes

Developing laser particles as intracellular photonic probes for cell labeling, imaging, and barcoding. Confocal imaging pipelines, flow cytometry correlation, and quantitative fluorescence analysis.

⬟

Semiconductor and quantum devices

Graphene–SiC quantum photonic devices for electrical control of quantum emitters in 4H-SiC. Hands-on with lithography, Pd contacts, van der Pauw structures, and transport characterization.

⊞

Computational image analysis

Building quantitative pipelines for confocal microscopy: tile handling, z-projection, Cellpose segmentation, LP localization, cell matching, and flow cytometry correlation.
